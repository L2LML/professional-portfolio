Root Cause Analysis

Tool / access friction

No per-student storage, no consistent writing surface, no pens.

Knowledge gap

One team member never formally trained on the ABC method.

No feedback loop

Data collected had no visible use — so it had no visible value.

Every starred step in the Lisa lane is a process change she personally introduced — not a step she merely performed. The steady-state process that follows (shared across the full team) exists because of those five interventions; the compliance and outcome numbers above are the measured result of them.

Lessons Learned — A Natural Comparison

Behavioral data (this process)

  • Same fixes applied: accessible tools, training, dated turn-in to one location.
  • BCBA actively reviewed the folder and used it to change a student's schedule.
  • Compliance sustained near 80% for the full period tracked.

IEP goal data (parallel effort, same team)

  • Same fixes applied, reached 80% compliance in month one.
  • No one reviewed or acted on the data afterward.
  • Compliance fell to 30%, then under 10%, within two more months — including Lisa's own recording frequency.

Takeaway: access and training raise compliance temporarily, but only a visible feedback loop — someone using the data and staff seeing that it mattered — sustains it. That held true even for the person most invested in the process.
